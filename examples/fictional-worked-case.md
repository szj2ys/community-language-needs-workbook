# Worked case: a community information session

**Everything in this case is invented.** The area, estimates, responses, languages selected and staff arrangements are teaching inputs. They are not Census extracts, a real survey, a product test or a report of community impact. `EXAMPLE-AREA` is deliberately not a real geographic code.

## The question

The fictional coordinator asks: “What Spanish-language support can we honestly announce for the next community information session?” The group can postpone the announcement until it checks one short written option. No actual meeting date or volunteer is implied.

The fictional numbers do not justify “120 people need Spanish interpretation.” Even with real data, home language and service demand would remain different concepts.

## Fill one listening-tally row

Use tally ID `T-01`, activity `fictional information session`, and method `optional paper card at an imagined planning desk`. The made-up collection received eight responses: five requested a Spanish written notice, two requested someone interpreting speech, and one selected “not sure.” Those are responses, not verified unique people.

In the real worksheet, create a separate row for each distinct request. Record coverage gaps: the invitation was offered only at one desk, did not reach absent people, and its reach across language varieties is unknown. Record possible repeat responses. The figures do not estimate what fraction of the neighborhood wants each option.

## First memo: a useful pending decision

The memo cites E-01 only as a teaching input, T-01 as the fictional listening tally and C-01/C-02 as capability checks. It says: **No Spanish-language support is ready to announce yet.** The written notice needs review; live interpreting has no confirmed provider. The next action belongs to the notice-coordinator role. The participant message must not imply that a translated notice is already available.

## Update only after new evidence

For a second exercise, assume explicitly that a fictional competent reviewer checks the notice's language variety, time, place and help instructions, and the coordinator confirms printed copies at the staffed help point. Record those assumed observations in C-01 and change only that option to `READY FOR THIS ACTIVITY`.

The revised memo can say “A reviewed Spanish notice will be available at the staffed help point,” within the fictional exercise. C-02 stays `NOT OFFERED`; the written notice does not turn into live interpreting. If the copies or help-point staffing are missing, reopen C-01. In a real plan, the corresponding observations must actually occur before the state changes.

## Check your finished memo

- Does it separate an area estimate, a response tally and delivery capability?
- Can a reader identify exactly which support is ready and which is not?
- Did a new observation justify each changed state?
- Did the memo avoid a claim about all residents or attendees?
- Is the public-facing message free of names, raw responses and small-group details?

The exercise is complete when the memo answers these questions clearly. It does not validate the method in a real community.
