# Community language needs workbook

An offline workbook for a library, community group or church deciding what language support it can offer. It keeps three kinds of evidence separate: **area estimates, what participants ask for, and what the team can actually provide**.

The result is a short, reviewable decision memo. This workbook does not translate, choose people's language for them, certify accessibility, or estimate demand from Census counts. It is original AI-assisted planning material, not a field-tested survey instrument or a report of a real community program.

## Start with one decision

Choose a narrow question, such as: “What language support can we reliably offer at next month's information session?” A broad goal such as “serve everyone in the area” does not tell the team what to check next.

1. **Record the setting and question.** Copy [the decision memo](templates/decision-memo.md) into a private working folder. Set a decision date and describe the activity, without listing participants.
2. **Describe the area evidence.** Use [the evidence register](templates/evidence-register.csv). Save the source URL, release/period, geographic type and code, population universe, original category label, estimate and its published margin. Leave unknown fields visibly unknown. Read [the field guide](field-guide.md) before interpreting the numbers.
3. **Ask about participation.** Use [the listening card](listening-card.md) to invite optional input. Record only a private aggregate tally in [the listening worksheet](templates/listening-tally.csv). This is a convenience sample of people who responded, not a population estimate.
4. **Check delivery.** In [the capability worksheet](templates/capability-check.csv), record the format and language variety, the responsible role, availability, rehearsal result and fallback. A willing volunteer is not yet a confirmed interpreting service.
5. **Make the limited decision.** In the memo, cite the evidence IDs, explain uncertainties and say what will be offered, what remains unavailable and when to reconsider. If a required capability is unconfirmed, keep that option pending. A small ready option is more useful than a large unsupported promise.

Read [the fully fictional worked case](examples/fictional-worked-case.md) once, then start with your own blank copies. Its numbers, location, responses and team arrangements are invented for instruction. They must not be quoted as research or local data.

## Use the files without an account

Use GitHub's **Code → Download ZIP**, unzip it, and open the Markdown documents in a text editor or read them on GitHub. Open each CSV in a spreadsheet using UTF-8 and comma separators. The CSV files contain headings only. Keep geographic codes as text so leading zeros survive; let the spreadsheet handle quoting. Do not paste formulas into shared text fields.

The method works on paper too: copy the headings and complete one row for each evidence item. There is no executable code, hosted form, analytics or data upload in this workbook. Your editor or storage provider may have its own data practices. Keep the completed workbook private; publish only a reviewed summary that cannot identify a participant.

## Optional lookup and scope

[PewTranslate's languages-spoken-by-ZIP-code lookup](https://pewtranslate.com/en/guides/languages-spoken-by-zip-code) is one way to explore US/Puerto Rico area estimates before checking the official source and recording its period, geography and limits. It is optional; the workbook also works with official Census tables and your own reviewed source records. It does not provide Brazilian or global demographic coverage.
