# An optional language-support listening card

Copy and adapt this invitation only after someone competent has reviewed the wording in the languages you plan to offer. The English prompts below are not approved translations or a validated questionnaire.

## Before inviting responses

- Name the activity and the decision the responses will inform.
- Explain what support is already available and what is only being considered.
- Decide who can view the private tally and when the working records will be removed under your group's normal process.
- Offer a way to respond without a smartphone. Check that the invitation itself reaches people who need language support.
- Do not require a name, email, phone number, precise address, age, nationality, membership status or explanation of a personal situation.

Do not call the collection anonymous merely because names are omitted. A rare language, detailed free-text answer or small group can identify someone. Keep small tallies private and avoid collecting stories or exact attendance times.

## Invitation text to adapt

> We are planning language support for [activity]. Answering is optional. Please do not include your name or other personal details. Your answers help us understand what would be useful, but they do not guarantee a service. The team will announce the support it can actually provide before the activity.

## Three prompts

1. **For this activity, what language or language variety would you prefer to receive information in?** You may leave this blank or write “no preference.” Do not guess a person's answer for them.
2. **What format would be useful?** For example: a short written notice, a full written outline, someone interpreting speech, or help asking a practical question. Offer only relevant options, allow “something else” and “not sure,” and do not suggest that a written summary supplies full interpreting.
3. **Is there a practical obstacle we should plan for?** For example: no smartphone or a need for a different print size. Ask for the obstacle only, not a diagnosis or personal history. A separate assistance route should handle private requests.

## Recording and closing the loop

Transfer only necessary aggregate counts into `templates/listening-tally.csv`. Count responses, not people, unless you have a justified method for establishing unique people. Do not attempt to deduplicate by collecting identities just to improve a planning tally.

Group similar requests only when that does not change their meaning. Preserve a language-variety distinction that matters for providing support. If a small group could be identified, keep the detail out of shared summaries. State the collection's limitations beside any count.

Tell participants, through the same accessible channels, what support the team will provide, what it cannot provide, and where to ask for help. Do not publish raw responses or the completed workbook in GitHub issues, pull requests or a public repository.
